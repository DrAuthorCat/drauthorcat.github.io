A Crimson Sky

Chapter 1: The Boy Who Would Be God

Chapter 1 of A Crimson Sky, The Boy Who Would Be God, by Josiah A. Ferrer.




Chris threw a ball against the wall, listening to the thump echo down the alley. He caught it, and threw it again. Of all the places to be, why here? He thought, the sweat was almost dripping from his hands. Maja was setting, but he knew the heat would last a few more hours. How long would he wait here, just to be stood up yet again? He was a young man, of age twenty two. Although the lack of sleep had aged him. Chris wore what most did around here, a brown cloak, with sun thread beneath. He blinked a few times. His hair was dark brown, parted in the middle to show his brown eyes. Despite the bags beneath them, and the way his eyes squinted there was still a look of naivety to him. A look that he was aware of, a look that got him into trouble and saved him from it. He caught the ball once more and then, the sounds of footsteps echoed down the alley. Three men approached, but only one spoke. The one in the middle, the only one with his face hidden behind a mask and desert glass. All were dressed the same, with faded brown robes. Chris could see his reflection in the man’s desert glass, he looked more tired than he remembered.

“Do you have it then?” The man said, his accent was odd, Chris had never heard someone speak in such a way.

“Yes.” Chris pulled the scroll from his cloak. Its red seal glimmered. 

“Hand it over, we’ll pay after we see the seals still intact.”  

“Use your eyes my friend,” Chris twirled the scroll in his hands. “Not fond of putting trust in strangers?”

Then there was a long pause, “And who trusts a thief?” The man laughed, before nodding to one of the men at his side. Chris took a step back, his eyes glancing to a barrel on his left. The biggest of the bunch, pulled a small leather bag out and shook it, the sound of coins clinking ringed in Chris’s ears. 

“Pay the trustworthy thief.” The man said, Chris could hear the smile that must have been beneath his face wrap. The henchman began walking to Chris. 

“Stop there, you can put it on the ground.” Chris said. The henchman looked back at his master, who gave him another nod. 

The coin purse hit the ground with a satisfying clank. Chris could already see the silver shimmer in his mind, the cool metal on his skin. “Few steps back,” he said, a smile on his face. The bigger man begrudgingly obeyed. Chris took a few steps forward and picked up the coins. The weight was just right, the sound, he took a sniff of the bag. 

“And the scroll.” The man said. It was odd, Chris still couldn't pin the accent.  

Chris placed the scroll down, and walked back. He was leaned up against the barrel as the men looked it over. He watched as they inspected it, knowing they’d find nothing. Chris smiled. 

“And no one saw you?” 

“Quiet as a mouse.”

“I need a more exact answer.”

Chris let out a breath, and stood as straight as he could. “No one saw me, my lord.” he said, annoyed.

“You talk a lot,”

“I promise, no one knows anything. No one will ever know.”

“Thank you.” The man took a long breath, his posture slanted in the slightest. “I would normally feel bad, but I think I’ll enjoy this.”

Chris felt the air turn cold, his breath paused. He turned to see another two men approaching from the other end of the alley.

“Do you have any clue what you held?” The man lifted the scroll to his eyes, Chris could see the smile peek out from beneath his mask.

“Lets talk lets just talk,” Chris said, as he looked back and forth, watching them approach him from both sides. He was surrounded.

“Go ahead. Scream.” The man said, with a snicker.

Chris pulled the barrel over, spilling water at his feet, he crouched down, eyes locked on that man behind the mask, then he closed his eyes and spoke. “Silent night, moon and mother, winter abyss.” he placed a hand an inch from the water's surface. His heart was racing, he was used to the adrenaline, but it was the fear that was new. He had to focus. A new fear, a fear unlike being caught, death, pure and quick death. He let out his held breath and focused on the water at his feet. He could feel the mana rushing through his arm like a stream. A coldness formed in his mind. His palm came down on the water, it flowed forward and up, then crackled and cracked into pure ice. Chris stumbled and looked out of breath as he opened his eyes. The wall of ice was massive, bigger than anything he’d tried to form before. It would hold, but there was still the other issue. He looked back to the two men still trapping him here. Now the odds were a little more fair he thought, the fear was gone now. He could do this. The men approached, blades drawn.

He could see the shimmer of silver or could it be bronze, he couldn't tell under this darkness. And the other had no weapon, this one was dangerous. Either he was mad enough to fight with his fists. Or, worse, he was a mage. 

The crackle of electricity lit the alley in a blue haze. Mage it was, Chris thought. He closed his eyes. Chris’s heart started to race, his inner eye was blinded for a moment by a blue aura, it was the lightning and its power was… death, quick and swift death.

He didn't have much time to think. He focused on the area between them, he focused his very energy into a wall, a wall of pure magic. It formed a thin blue sheet, a wall of magical glass. The lightning whipped from the mages hand and crashed into the wall, it shattered. He opened his eyes. Shards of blue mana rained down before him, he could see the mage conjuring more lightning, and the other-. A dagger hit his chest, he looked down at it. A second bolt of lightning whipped through the air and came barreling at him but it dispersed halfway to him. The mage had over exerted himself, but Chris hadn't even noticed. He was bleeding and in more pain then he had thought possible. He placed a hand on the dagger in his chest, the pain made moving impossible.  

“Can we talk?” Chris mumbled. 

He fell down to one knee, he felt cold. Why can't I move, I, I’m dying. He thought. Blood spewed down onto the alley's floor. How could he die here? Why? He was just a thief, he was just a boy.  

A memory came to him, one of annoyance. It was his father, ‘everyone gets hurt now and then, but a good mage is a healthy one, that's why-’

‘I don't care dad. That's boring magic! Teach me something cooler!’

‘Son.’ He remembered the look, the disappointment in his fathers eyes. It was more vivid now then it had ever been. ‘Fine, I'll teach you something else.’ The red haze of flames fluttered on his fathers face. He could see the color so clearly, yet, the face’s details evaded him. 

The memory was gone, his vision was blurred. Reality quickly rushed back in. Blood. I’m losing too much blood. The pain was too much, but he had to survive, he had to. He pulled the dagger from his skin, the pain made his vision go black for a moment, in one ripping tear. He had to cast magic, but he could see them, still approaching. He closed his eyes, imbued the dagger and threw it. While it flew he focused on the pain in his stomach, the point. 

He let out a grunted breath, the memory came back to him fully now, ‘Conjuration, palm of ember.’ From his hand came pure fire, he placed it on the wound, the pain brought him to his knees. When he opened his eyes, his wound was sealed. They’d dodged the dagger, soon they’d be on him. But now the dagger was behind them, and still imbued with Chris’s mana.

He lifted his hand and closed his eyes. “Gravitation, hidden art, blessed by the moon, pull.” The words gave the spell even more power, he felt it flow through him. A small orb formed in front of Chris, almost like a moon, the men wondered what it was, a projectile? Their thoughts didn't matter, by the time they understood the spell, it was too late. The two men tried to look back as the dagger twisted through the air and slammed into the back of the mage's neck. He fell to the ground motionless. The other one looked in horror and stopped in his tracks. It was the first time Chris had killed a man, but only one thought was on his mind, survival. ‘Kill or be killed.’ Chris looked to his ice wall and pulled a thin sharp slice. The last one watched the dagger fly through the air he readied to block, ice stabbed into his heart. Chris walked out of the alley, a sway to his steps.

A tiredness like none he’d ever known washed over him, but he couldn't stop. He had to keep moving. The streets were crowded, traders and beggars flowed like rivers. He stumbled from person to person, most ignored him. They would be closing in, if they wanted him dead they wouldn't give up so easily. He had to keep. Moving. ‘Find him!’ it was that man’s voice. His body thumped to the ground, dreaming and dying in the street.
