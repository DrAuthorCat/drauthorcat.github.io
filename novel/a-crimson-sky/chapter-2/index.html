A Crimson Sky

Chapter 2: The Young Prince

Chapter 2 of A Crimson Sky, The Young Prince, by Josiah A. Ferrer.




Three loud knocks woke Ryan from his dream, he gasped in air. Sweat clinging to his skin. He tried to remember the dream, but he could not. Cool air rushed in through his window, and he could hear the gentle song of the birds. Another three loud knocks. Slowly, he rose from his bed, putting on just enough clothes to answer the door, a white shirt and pants. With a sigh he swung it open, his uncle met him, standing there with royal guards at his side. “Ryan.” His uncle said, his eyes were wet and his face hung down. He looked young if not for the skin beneath his eyes, the only part that showed his true age.

Ryan could feel the world lose its balance for a moment, as if he were a breath from falling. “Uncle?” Ryan said, swallowing his momentary fear. “What,” He tried to speak more but the words would not leave his lips.

His uncle tried, his mouth opened, but there was no response. Instead there was a sentence spoken with his eyes. A stare of guilt and shame, a look that could mean only one thing: his face scrunched up and there was the hint of a frown held back only by a thin string. He took in a breath, and to Ryan the silence felt as if it would never end.

“Your father…” His uncle said, there was a storm just beneath his words, a subtle wave to his tone. “Ryan, he is gone.” 

The words were no surprise, he had expected them again and again. He had thought of this exact moment more times than he could count, but it was different. No matter how hard and how vivid Ryan had imagined it, the tears, the feeling. It was only the emptiness he could feel. Even as he blinked, he could see his father laying there in bed, still as a stone. And no matter how horrid the nightmare of his imagination was, all it took was simply opening his eyes to escape but now, there was no escape, his eyes had opened to the dream. 

Ryan hadn't noticed but his uncle had broken into tears, still he kept that frown restrained, held back by years of practice. It still felt like a dream, as if all he needed was to open his eyes. Should he be crying? He wondered. But the tears eluded him. He slipped past his uncle and started walking down the hall, the steps of the guard following close behind. “Ryan!” His uncle ran to his side, “He is still being prepared by the Magea, we must wait.” His voice cracked.

Ryan paid no mind to his words as he kept his pace. Three more doors. Ahead he could see people gathered, guards, magea, shuffling through the halls like fish in a pond. Two more doors. His steps slowed, and the hall seemed longer than it had ever been and for just a second he felt a spark of joy, but it was only a second. For a moment the world reminded him of a dream he’d had, one of this very day and in that second he felt relief. But in that dream he hadn't been walking, he was running and the hall had stretched out on for as far as he could see, he ran and ran but it just kept going, he could recall the gasp of air he’d taken in as he woke up from that nightmare. But as his eyes locked on the end of the hall, he knew it wasn't the same dream. He had stopped. He could hear the shuffling steps of the Magea, the servants. He watched the guards as they stood in silence around the end of the hall. Just ahead of him was his fathers door. “Ryan.” His uncle put a hand on his shoulder.

“How? How do I know he's dead?” Ryan said. The hall carried his words and he saw a few stop to look before quickly averting their eyes. “I, I can’t just wait.” Finally Ryan turned to face his uncle. The old man’s eyes were a dark green, so dark one might mistake it for brown. His skin was well kept, except for the bags beneath his eyes, the price paid for long nights. His chin was rounded and he had a youth to him. Often at gatherings he’d been mistaken for Ryan’s brother. They looked similar, except for their hair. Ryan had short curly black hair, and his uncle had a long long tried back in a pony tail.

“I know Ryan but it isn't right, I-” He took in a gasp of air, holding back a flood of tears. “I want to see him too, but we need to respect the proper way. It is what he would have wanted.”

Ryan took a moment to bask in his words, “Proper? What if he isn't dead? How can you know for sure? Will you trust the Magea? Do you?”

“I-I do.” His uncle said, a sureness to his words.

“Well.” Ryan let out a sigh. “I don't," and he walked forward. Eyes followed him as he walked through the crowd. He was only a few steps from the door. Surrounded by people he didn't even know, people who couldn't be trusted. Of all the lessons his father had taught him, one his father valued above the rest. They were words to live by, ‘Do not trust them.’

When he entered the room, it fell silent. The Magea were the only ones inside, their silk face clothes swaying in front of their eyes. They all looked at him, as if they were a single person. Suddenly he felt the urge to retreat, but it was far too late. His eyes turned to the bed, where silently a corpse lay. “Out.” He said, but the word came only as a whisper. “Out!” All listened, and soon the room was empty. He walked over to his fathers side, there was a cloth over his face. Ryan lifted it, to see his father there, eyes open staring up at the ceiling. “No…” The word was nothing more than a murmur. Ryan dropped to his knees behind the bed, and wrapped his arms around the corpse. It was cold. He expected it then, as someone might expect a slap. He thought the tears would come then, but they did not, instead he felt an emptiness. The room was quiet. 

He had hated it, the way the sickness had taken to his fathers lungs, making his breaths ghostly and ragged. He had felt guilty for the way his fathers breaths had irritated him. But now in the silence, all he wished was to hear them again, to simply be annoyed. Then, the patter of rain began, he could hear the gentle drops hitting the window. He stared out, watching as the water slid down the glass. He looked back to the corpse, it all still felt like a dream. He looked at his father’s face, studying the details. The patches of hair where once long black hair had flourished, his eyes grey and glazed over, the skin that seemed to be dripping off of him. He let out a breath from his nose and smiled, he could never look at his father this long, without the man growing angry, Ryan could never understand why, but now he did. He could see himself grow old, losing his hair, his teeth, how dreadful it must be to become a corpse while still alive. 

   

.   .   .




Rain was pouring down, there was a child standing behind blue glass, watching the rain dance down the window. There was a single step’s sound that made the boy turn his head. His eyes were white, pure white unlike a blind man's. They were eyes that could see more, then most could imagine. Before the child was a man in black robes, he was frozen, a dagger glinting in his hands. This child wasn't normal. He knew this wouldn't be the usual assassination. But a child's a child, it couldn't be that hard. “Hey now, don't scream. I just want to play a game.” His voice was a calming whisper. 

The boy lifted his palm, and looked at the man. It was the man who spoke, “Hey lets just-” 

In the next moment, the man’s body was turned into ash, it was quicker then he could react. Flames spewed out the room and blew out stone walls. It was an explosion of power. The boy stood in the rubble, unbothered by the guards that must have died, the servants, they died the moment they let an assassin so close. And regardless, they were only human. Humans died all the time. But he was sad now, the window had broken from the pressure. He knew he could form it again, manipulate the glass into the same shape, but what use would it be. The rain had stopped. He looked up and saw the sky’s blue above him.
