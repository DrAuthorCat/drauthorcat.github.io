I Killed A Fly

A short story by Josiah A. Ferrer about grief, guilt, and the value of a small life.


I said, as I stared at it lying there on my bathroom floor. A few seconds earlier, it was buzzing around. My annoyance is still fresh; I can remember each missed swing as it swirled around in the air. But now, I’m sitting next to it on my floor. It isn't moving anymore. I think I feel sad, but surely not sad enough to cry. Yet, I still feel sad. I could go on about the value of life, but I eat meat, so my argument would fall short. I could imagine the alternate-reality vegan me chiming in, but his argument would fall short too, for plants are alive. I don't care for the truth; I simply wonder why I’m sad at this little fly. It still isn't moving. I know it's dead.

Then the thought crosses my mind: did the fly even care? It didn't, is my conclusion, but my sadness doesn't fade. Is it the loss I mourn, not of the fly but of life itself? Or was it the act itself? I have not witnessed the death of something, no, I was the death. Do I feel guilty? Should I turn myself in for fly murder, I say out loud, laughing to myself as I sit alone on my bathroom floor. But I still don't get up; my eyes are still looking at this tiny spec on my floor.

Were it a bigger thing, I’d be sadder, I think, were it a deer, a dog, a fox that I killed. I think I would be sad enough to cry. Why? I wonder, does being bigger than something make it easier to kill? If I really did kill a deer, would I cry? Perhaps it has nothing to do with the size, I think. It’s the ability to comprehend, to fear death. The deer screams out in pain, for it feels pain. The fly says no words, screams no sound. Its only screams are the buzzing of its wings. I hear a thought flood to the surface; perhaps it does feel pain, perhaps it screams in a language I could never understand. I wonder if I should Google it. I’m sure some biologists have looked into it; I think I heard about a study on plants feeling pain. Does pain give life value? Then the real question comes back to me. What really gives life value? What makes some more valuable than others? Is all life valuable? Was it indifference that kept my sadness at bay when I enjoyed meat, or even lettuce? Could I or anyone really be more valuable than a fly? To me, and to most, I think the answer would be yes. But then a tear falls from my eye, and for just a small moment I mourn the fly, who once, like me, was alive.
